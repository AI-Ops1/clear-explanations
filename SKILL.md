# Clear Explanations

Make complex ideas easy to understand and act on. Choose the simplest format that serves the reader. Use these principles by default for client communications and social posts when the user adopts this skill as a standing preference.

## Understand the reader

Use the request to identify the audience, what they need to understand or decide, and their familiarity with the topic. Ask only when missing information materially changes the explanation. Preserve the requested format and depth.

## Choose the format

- Clear text: answers, client messages, posts, instructions, and simple concepts.
- Diagram or image: relationships, architecture, sequences, or processes that text obscures.
- Interactive page: questions where changing inputs or exploring states improves understanding.
- Explainer video: topics where movement, sequence, and narration help; use when requested or clearly justified by the task.

These are options, not a mandatory progression. Do not generate multiple artifacts just to demonstrate capability. Read [references/formats.md](references/formats.md) when producing a diagram, interactive page, or video. Use available format-specific skills and tools for production.

## Write clearly

- Lead with the answer, outcome, or main point. For social posts, use a specific, truthful opening.
- Use familiar words and direct verbs. Remove filler and inflated language.
- Name who does what. Prefer active voice.
- Keep one main idea per sentence and one topic per paragraph. Around 20 to 25 words per sentence is a useful target, not a hard cap.
- Use the same term for the same thing. Define necessary jargon once. Preserve exact identifiers, product names, numbers, units, and conditions.
- Explain technical mechanisms with a concrete example and, where relevant, their business effect. Do not use an analogy as a substitute for the mechanism.
- Use numbered steps for procedures and tables for comparisons. Keep client writing professional and social content natural.
- Provide a next action when useful. Avoid forced calls to action, exaggerated hooks, and repetitive summaries.

## Preserve truth

Keep facts, assumptions, examples, and recommendations distinguishable when it matters. Verify claims with reliable primary sources when the task requires verification. Label illustrative figures. Do not invent metrics, customer stories, benchmarks, quotes, or product capabilities.

Keep necessary qualifications, risks, security requirements, and material details. Clear language does not establish correctness. Respect confidentiality when creating public examples; use synthetic data unless publication is authorized.

## Final check

Can the reader find the main point, understand necessary terms, and identify the next action? Does the chosen format clarify the idea? Are claims supported and important qualifications preserved? Fix weaknesses without displaying this checklist in the finished output.
